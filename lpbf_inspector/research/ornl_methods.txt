Build Design
A laser powder bed fusion build was completed on a Colibrium Additive M2 using stainless steel 316H powder. Four types of components were constructed: (1) nominal cylinders, (2) high volumetric energy density (VED) cylinders, (3) spatter generators, and (4) a trigger cylinder. First, 56 nominal cylinders (approximately 15 mm in diameter and 4 in tall) were printed with parameters known from post-build XCT scans to produce material with densities in excess of 99.99%. Next, 8 cylinders with the same dimensions as the nominal cylinders were printed high volumetric energy density (VED) process parameters. All cylinders were designed such that fatigue coupons conforming to ASTM E466 could be excised. Two spatter generation blocks were printed upstream of the cylinders such that they would seed spatter particles across the 64 cylindrical specimens.  Half of the cylinders were located in four rows just downstream of the spatter generators, while the remaining cylinders were placed in four rows not downstream of the spatter generators. Finally, a trigger cylinder was printed to aid with data acquisition stability.

The DMSCNN was trained to detect the following anomaly classes:

Powder: regions of unfused powder within the powder bed.
Printed: regions of powder fused by the laser beam
Recoater Hopping: interactions between the recoating mechanism and the powder bed can cause variances in the layer thickness, perpendicular to the travel direction.
Recoater Streaking: if the recoating mechanism is damaged or dragging a large particle, it can create a streak across the powder bed that changes the effective powder thickness on top of parts
Incomplete Spreading: occurs if an insufficient amount of powder is dosed across the powder bed
Powder Mounding: mounds of powder dropped onto the powder bed by the recoater blade
Debris: these anomalies are typically caused by a delamination or severe warping followed by the recoater striking a part.
Spatter on Powder: spatter particles ejected from the melt pool and condensed metal vapor can appear as diffuse, dark clouds in the gas flow direction.
Swelling: certain local geometries (e.g., corners and edges) can cause melt pools to increase in size, distorting the part geometry and allowing it to stick out above the powder layer.
Super-Elevation: sections of a part sticking up above the powder bed due to either a delamination event or a short feed.
Misprint: occasionally, a printer will melt an unintended geometry. This can be detected as a misprint by comparing the printed detections to the corresponding *.stl.
Stripe Boundary: stripe boundaries occur at the intersection between scan blocks. The laser turnarounds at boundaries can change the porosity populations and thermal conditions.
Over Melting: parts melted with high energy density process parameters that might be more likely to produce keyholing pores.
Under Melting: parts melted with low energy density process parameters that might be more likely to produce lack of fusion pores.
Localized Bright Spot: small bright spots in the TI-NIR images thought to correspond to stochastic spatter particles
Localized Dark Spot: small dark spots in the TI-NIR images, possibly due to topographical changes in the printed surface
Dropped NIR Data: this is not an anomaly, but an imaging artifact that occurs when an integrated NIR camera system fails to capture one or more frames of data during melting.
Pitting: small, localized dark regions in the post-recoat images thought to be caused by small perturbations in the recoated powder layer

Fatigue Testing
Of the 64 cylinders manufactured, 60 were selected for fatigue testing following XCT scanning. These cylinders were machined into fatigue coupons conforming to ASTM E466 and then subjected to fatigue testing with a stress ratio of 0.1 at 30 Hz. Half of the coupons were tested at a maximum stress level of 300 MPa, which generally falls within the range of high cycle fatigue tests for additively manufactured stainless steels. The other half of the coupons were tested at a maximum stress level of 450 MPA, which corresponds to low cycle fatigue testing.
